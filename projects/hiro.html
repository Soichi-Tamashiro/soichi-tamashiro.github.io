Context

Peru sits on a highly seismic coastline where a major offshore earthquake can send a tsunami ashore within minutes. Japan's EWBS (Emergency Warning Broadcast System) embeds alert data inside the regular digital-TV broadcast signal — but there was no locally-made device able to receive and act on it. HIRO was a joint ELOGIC–PUCP R&D project, funded by Innóvate Perú, to build one.

The challenge

  • Decode an alert buried in a broadcast signal and turn it into a reliable, immediate local warning.
  • Run on cheap, deployable hardware so the receiver could realistically reach coastal communities.
  • Bridge two worlds — broadcast/RF engineering and software — that rarely share a codebase.

What I did

  • Co-developed HIRO end-to-end — the first tsunami early-warning receiver built in Peru, able to alert up to 20 minutes before coastal impact.
  • Prototyped on embedded platforms (Raspberry Pi, ARM, Arduino), bridging broadcast engineering and software.
  • Published the work in IEEE Xplore and presented it as a real, demonstrable device — not just a paper.

Impact & recognition

HIRO was recognized as the first tsunami-alert reception device made in Peru, published in IEEE Xplore and covered by El Comercio. It demonstrated a viable, low-cost path to earlier coastal warnings.
